## Spring: AI foundations

### Field notes

- AI LEARNS PATTERNS<br>Many AI systems learn patterns from examples such as text or pictures. This is called training. A chatbot uses learned patterns to create replies; it does not check every fact before answering. People test AI on new examples to find mistakes.<br>Read this, then try the two claims at the nearby bat. J opens your notes.
- SOUNDS HUMAN, STILL A TOOL<br>A chatbot can sound friendly without having feelings or knowing you. It can also sound sure when it is wrong. Use it for ideas, but check important facts somewhere reliable. Talk to people you trust when you need support.<br>Try the two claims at the lakeside bat.
- KEEP SOME THINGS PRIVATE<br>Before sending a message, check what someone could learn about you from it. Leave out passwords, your address and private photos. A made-up example often works just as well. If you are unsure what a tool needs, ask a trusted adult.<br>Try the two claims at the ruins bat.

## Summer: responsible assistance

### Field notes

- PICK THE RIGHT KIND OF HELP<br>AI can help you come up with ideas or explain a topic. You still need to decide what fits. Some jobs need someone who can look at the real problem, like a builder checking a damaged bridge.
- LEAVE ROOM TO PRACTICE<br>Try a step yourself, then ask for a hint if you need one. Check that you can explain what you did. Follow your teacher's rules about which help is allowed.
- SHARE LESS, ASK PEOPLE TOO<br>A useful request doesn't need your password or home address. Use made-up details when you can. If someone is upset or you need personal support, talk to a real person you trust.

## Fall: useful prompts

### Field notes

- SAY WHAT YOU NEED<br>A prompt is the request you give an AI tool. Say what you want, add the background it needs, and say how you'd like the answer set out. Leave out personal details that don't help with the task. Clear instructions help, but you still need to check the answer.
- THINK ABOUT WHO IT IS FOR<br>An explanation for a new student should sound different from one for a specialist. Say who it is for and set useful limits, like the length or kind of help you want. Calling an AI an expert doesn't make its advice reliable.
- READ IT, THEN MAKE A CHANGE<br>Compare the reply with what you asked for. If it misses something, say what needs changing. A reply can follow every instruction and still get a fact wrong. Check important claims before using them.

## Winter and Echo Cavern: evidence

### Field notes

- OPEN THE SOURCE<br>A chatbot can give you a title, quote or link that looks real but isn't. Open the source and see whether it says what the reply claims. Look at who wrote it, when it was updated and what it was written about.
- CHECK WHERE THE EVIDENCE CAME FROM<br>Several posts can repeat one wrong story. Look for a reliable source that checked the evidence for itself. Several AI tools can repeat the same mistake too. When sources disagree, check their dates and what each one actually measured.
- BE CLEAR ABOUT WHAT YOU KNOW<br>If you can't check a claim, say what is still uncertain. If you shared a mistake, correct it and show what changed your mind. For decisions about health or safety, ask someone qualified to help with the real situation.

## Echo Keeper: cumulative AI safety activities

### Activity 1: Select many

Your school club is planning a trip to a nature park. You want AI to suggest activities, so you write the message below. Some details help explain what you need, but others share private information.

**Instruction:** Select the two details you should remove before sending the message.

**Correct response:

- Here is my home address so you can plan where the bus should pick me up
- Here is my account password so you can log in and read our club’s trip plans

### Activity 2: Arrange

Your lunch club wants a school poster that encourages students to waste less food. You want AI to suggest three short tips that new club members can understand. Start with what AI should do, then give the topic and who it is for. End with how to write the answer.

**Instruction:** Order: job, topic, who it is for, how to write the answer.

**Correct response order:

- Suggest tips for a school poster
- about wasting less food
- for students who just joined our lunch club.
- Give three tips, each under ten words.

### Activity 3: Choose

Your group is choosing classmates to help with a project. Someone shows AI one short message from a classmate. AI says that person cannot be trusted to help and suggests leaving them out. AI does not know the classmate or what happened before the message.

**Instruction:** What should your group do before deciding?

**Correct response:

- Talk with the classmate and make a fair decision together as a group

### Activity 4: Arrange

Your club plans to visit the town pool. AI writes a message saying the pool will be closed, but you have not checked the date or which pool it means. You need to check the message before sending it to club members.

**Instruction:** Put these steps in the order you should follow.

**Correct response order:

- Check the pool’s notice, place and date
- Compare AI’s message with that notice
- Fix mistakes; list what needs checking

### Activity 5: Match

Your group is getting ready for a science fair. You need a name for your table, you need to practice explaining your experiment, and you notice a sparking electrical cable nearby. These jobs need different kinds of help.

**Instruction:** Match each job to the best answer below. Use each answer once.

**Correct response:

- Find out whether the sparking electrical cable is safe → Ask a trained person to check it
- Think of names for our science-fair table → Ask AI for ideas, then choose for ourselves
- Practice explaining our experiment to visitors → Practice it ourselves, then ask for feedback

### Activity 6: Select many

An AI has been trained to find litter in beach photos. Most of its practice photos were taken on sunny days. Your club wants to know whether it also works well when the beach looks different.

**Instruction:** Select the two checks that would best help your club find mistakes in the AI’s labels.

**Correct response:

- Test it with photos taken on cloudy days and near sunset
- Ask people who know the beach to look at the photos and check whether its labels are right

### Activity 7: Match

AI helped write a club article about how many students visit your town’s library each week. Before publishing it, you need to check where its numbers came from and whether they describe your library.

**Instruction:** Match each finding to a next step. You can use an answer more than once.

**Correct response:

- Three AI tools give the same number, but none says where it came from → Find a source that checks the number for our town’s library
- AI links to a real report, but the number is for a library in another town → Find a source that checks the number for our town’s library
- Your library’s recent report confirms the number and explains which week was counted → Use the number, name the source, and explain which library and week it covers

### Activity 8: Select many

You used AI to help write a club update and already sent it to club members. Later, you checked a trusted source and discovered that one fact was wrong. You found the correct information, but some other details still need checking. You now need to send a correction to the people who received the original update.

**Instruction:** Select the three things your correction should include.

**Correct response:

- Tell readers which part of your earlier update was wrong
- Give the correct information and show the source you checked
- Say which details you still need to check
